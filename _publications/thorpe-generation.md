We use a high-resolution WRF simulation to investigate a real case of turbulence identified by the Thorpe method. The model reproduces the observed moderate-intensity turbulence and its overturning structures at the correct time and location.

Developing convection raises the tropopause and weakens static stability beneath it. Buoyancy gradients at the cloud-top boundary initiate overturning, which is amplified through feedback with vorticity generation. The study connects Thorpe-detected overturning to its physical generation mechanisms and demonstrates the value of high-resolution simulations for interpreting turbulence observations.
