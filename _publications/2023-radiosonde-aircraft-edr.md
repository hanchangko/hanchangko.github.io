We compare turbulence intensity estimated from high-resolution radiosonde data with commercial aircraft measurements along major U.S. flight routes over six years.

Both datasets identify elevated turbulence occurrence over the Rocky Mountains, but their altitude and seasonal patterns differ. These differences reflect sampling limitations and the types and scales of turbulence captured by each approach, including limitations of Thorpe-based estimates in detecting shear instability within statically stable layers. The comparison clarifies how radiosondes can complement aircraft observations and where interpretation requires care.
