We examine how stratospheric aerosol injection could affect aviation turbulence using simulations from three climate models under multiple climate and geoengineering scenarios.

In the scenarios studied, geoengineering counteracts the projected increase in turbulence and reduces its occurrence below present-day levels. The size of the reduction varies across models and intervention strategies. These results identify a potential aviation-related effect of climate intervention, while emphasizing that it must be considered alongside broader risks and uncertainties.
