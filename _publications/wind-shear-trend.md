We analyze twenty years of high-resolution radiosonde observations from 68 stations across the contiguous United States to examine changes in upper-tropospheric and lower-stratospheric vertical wind shear.

Fine-scale wind shear shows stronger increases than suggested by bulk-layer estimates and ERA5 reanalysis, with maximum shear increasing by 7.4% per decade. The results show how coarse vertical resolution can obscure changes in intense shear and suggest that aviation turbulence risks may increase more than large-scale shear estimates alone indicate.
